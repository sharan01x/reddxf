xfm is a minimal, distraction-free music player focused on the listening experience. Clean interface, no unnecessary features — just you and your music.

**What it is.** A local music player built around a single commitment: the music is the interface. It plays the files you already own — no store, no streaming catalogue, no account — and puts play, queue, and library within one glance's reach. Everything else that clutters modern players was either removed or never added.

**The problem it solves.** Music players stopped being music players. They became storefronts with a playback feature bolted on: ads, upsells, algorithmic feeds, social features nobody asked for, and a playback screen buried under commerce. For people who own their music files and just want them played well, every mainstream option gets in the way. xfm does the one job.

**Who it's for.** Listeners with a local library — collectors, audiophiles, anyone who ripped their CDs or buys from Bandcamp — and anyone who wants a player that opens instantly, plays what's selected, and stays out of the way.

**How it's built.** A lightweight application reading your local library directly: no import rituals, no cloud sync, no telemetry. The interface design follows the same discipline as the Redd Group's web work — clear type, sensible hierarchy, and restraint — because "simple" and "cheap-looking" are different things, and most minimal players settle for the second.

**A concrete use case.** You get home, open xfm, and your library is there exactly as it was — no login, no "discover" tab between you and the album you sat down to hear. Hit play. That's the whole story, and it's the point.

**Design note.** Every feature that exists earns its place; the test for adding anything new is whether it serves listening. If it serves engagement metrics instead, it doesn't get built.
